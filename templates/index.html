Behind every forgotten name
is a life fully lived

Alzheimer's and dementia do not define the people they touch. These are their stories — told with dignity, honoured with science.

Glen Campbell 75 — Country Music Legend, Arkansas
“
I'm not gonna let it stop me. If I mess up a lyric, people are gonna say Glen's forgot the words — but that's okay. I've never been perfect, and I've never pretended to be.

Glen Campbell — the voice behind "Rhinestone Cowboy" and "Wichita Lineman" — was one of the best-selling music artists of the 20th century. In June 2011, aged 75, he publicly announced a diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease, a moment of extraordinary courage at a time when stigma often kept such diagnoses private. Rather than retreat, he embarked on a 151-date "Goodbye Tour" across the United States and the United Kingdom.

Accompanied by his family on stage every night, Glen performed even as the disease advanced, sometimes forgetting lyrics mid-song but never losing the warmth that defined his artistry. The 2014 documentary I'll Be Me captured his journey — and helped change the national conversation about Alzheimer's. He passed away in August 2017, but his openness gave millions of families the language to talk about a condition they had long faced in silence.
